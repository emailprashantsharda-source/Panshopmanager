What a car rental business is

A car rental business lends cars to customers for a fee, by the hour, day or week. In India it comes in two forms: self-drive, where the customer drives the car, and car hire with a driver (taxis and cabs), which is a different licence and a different business. This guide is about self-drive car rental — the kind run by small operators in tourist towns and cities, often alongside bike and scooty rentals.

The business is simple to describe and hard to run well: you earn only when a car is out, every car carries fixed costs whether it is rented or not, and one careless customer can cost you a month's profit. The rest of this guide is about controlling those three things.

Licences and paperwork

Self-drive car rental is regulated under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. The central framework is the Rent a Cab Scheme, 1989, which each state adopts and runs through its transport department. Before you buy a single car, go to your State Transport Authority or RTO and ask for the current self-drive (rent-a-cab) rules in your state.

What the 1989 scheme text says (as published by the Andhra Pradesh transport department): the licence holder must have permits for at least 50 motor cabs, half of them air-conditioned; an office with parking and repair facilities; a telephone reachable round the clock; and every car with a valid permit, comprehensive insurance, fitness certificate and paid motor vehicle tax. The licence is valid for five years. States have changed these conditions over time, so treat this as background and confirm the rules that apply to you today.

Paperwork checklist

  • Rent-a-cab (self-drive) licence from your state transport department
  • Each car registered and permitted for rental use, with fitness certificate and road tax paid
  • Comprehensive insurance on every car
  • Business registration (proprietorship, partnership or company) and a current bank account
  • GST registration once your turnover crosses the services threshold — ₹20 lakh a year in most states (GST portal). Ask your CA which GST rate applies to your rentals.
  • A written rental agreement for every booking — you can make one with our free rental agreement generator

Renting out bikes and scooties is covered by a separate Rent a Motorcycle Scheme in many states (for example Himachal Pradesh and Andhra Pradesh).

How much money you need

There is no single number: it depends on how many cars you start with, which cars, and whether you pay cash or take a loan. Build your own cost sheet with these lines, one column per car:

Is it profitable? A worked example

For example, suppose one car rents for ₹2,000 a day and is rented out on 18 days in a month. These numbers are only an illustration — use your own.

Per car, per monthAmount
Rent earned (18 days × ₹2,000)₹36,000
Loan EMI− ₹15,000
Insurance (monthly share)− ₹2,500
Servicing and repairs− ₹3,000
Cleaning, parking and staff share− ₹2,000
Marketing and commissions− ₹1,500
Left over before your own salary and tax₹12,000

The fixed costs in this example add up to ₹24,000 a month. At ₹2,000 a day, the car must be rented out on 12 days a month just to break even (₹24,000 ÷ ₹2,000). Every day above 12 is profit; every day below is a loss. Track this number for each car every month — it tells you when to cut prices, move a car to a busier location, or sell it.

Choosing your cars

  • Rent what your customers ask for. Tourists, families, and people travelling for work want different cars. Ask local hotels and travel agents what their guests need before you buy.
  • Running cost matters more than purchase price. A car that is cheap to service, easy to find parts for, and economical on fuel keeps more of each rental as profit.
  • Manual or automatic? Offering both widens your customer base; automatics suit city renters who rarely drive manual cars.
  • Start small and add cars on proof. Add a car only when your existing cars are consistently above their break-even days.

Pricing: per day, per km, deposits

Most self-drive operators combine a daily rent with a kilometre limit. For example: ₹2,000 a day including 200 km, then ₹10 per extra km. A customer who drives 320 km in one day pays 120 km × ₹10 = ₹1,200 on top of the rent.

  • Record the odometer at pickup and return, every time, with a photo. Without both readings you cannot bill extra kilometres fairly.
  • Security deposit: take a refundable deposit at pickup and refund it after inspection. Keep deposits separate from your earnings — the money is not yours yet.
  • Late returns: set an hourly late charge and write it into the agreement.
  • Fuel: the simplest policy is "return with the same fuel level", noted at pickup.

Getting bookings

  • Google Business Profile: most local customers search "self drive car rental near me". A complete profile with photos, prices and real reviews is the cheapest source of bookings.
  • WhatsApp: share availability and rates quickly, and send bills after each rental.
  • Hotels, homestays and travel agents: agree a referral fee per booking.
  • Repeat customers: keep every customer's name, number and rental history, and message them before holidays and long weekends.
  • Booking platforms bring volume but take a commission; count it in your break-even sum.

Running it day to day

At pickup

  1. Check the customer's driving licence and note its number.
  2. Photograph the car on all sides and note existing scratches on the agreement.
  3. Record odometer and fuel level.
  4. Take the deposit and get the agreement signed.

At return

  1. Inspect the car against the pickup photos.
  2. Record odometer and fuel; work out extra km, late hours and fuel.
  3. Settle the deposit and send the bill.

Every week

  • Check each car's insurance, permit, fitness and PUC expiry dates — an expired paper can mean a fine or a car off the road.
  • Review each car's rented days against its break-even days.

Car rental business plan outline

If you need a business plan for a bank loan or a partner, cover these sections:

  1. Summary: what you rent, where, and to whom.
  2. Market: who rents cars in your city (tourists, business travellers, locals) and which operators already serve them.
  3. Licences: what your state requires and where you are in getting it.
  4. Fleet plan: which cars, how many, bought or financed.
  5. Pricing: daily rates, km limits, deposits, late charges.
  6. Costs and break-even: the cost sheet and break-even days per car from this guide.
  7. Marketing: how you will get the first 100 bookings.
  8. Operations: pickup and return process, maintenance, insurance claims.
  9. Funding: how much you need, from where, and when it is repaid.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start a car rental business?

It depends mostly on how many cars you start with and whether you buy them outright or on a loan. Add the licence, insurance, registration, parking and a cash buffer for repairs. Work it out car by car with the cost sheet in this guide.

Do I need a licence for a self-drive car rental business?

Yes. Self-drive car rental is licensed by your state's transport department under its Rent a Cab Scheme, and the cars must be registered and permitted for this use. Requirements such as minimum fleet size vary by state, so check with your State Transport Authority before you buy cars.

Is a car rental business profitable?

It can be, if each car is rented out on enough days to cover its fixed monthly costs. The break-even example in this guide shows how to find that number for your own cars.

Do I need GST registration?

Renting is a service, so registration is needed once your turnover crosses the services threshold — ₹20 lakh a year in most states, lower in some special category states. Ask your CA which GST rate applies to your rentals.

Can I rent out bikes too?

Yes, but motorcycle rentals have their own scheme in many states (the Rent a Motorcycle Scheme). RentalNow handles bikes, scooties and cars in one fleet list.
