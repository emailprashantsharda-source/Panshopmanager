Best Scooty and Bike for a Rental Business in India

Which scooty or bike should you buy first? Compare the Honda Activa, TVS Jupiter, Suzuki Access 125 and the Royal Enfield 350s on price, mileage, storage and what they earn in rent.

In short: start with 110cc scooties (TVS Jupiter or Honda Activa), add one Suzuki Access 125 for taller riders, and add a 350cc Royal Enfield only if tourists or long-trip riders are your customers. Check the figures with your dealer: prices and daily rates below were read from public pages on 5 November 2026 and change by city and season.

The short answer

There is no single best vehicle. A rental scooty earns from being rented on as many days as possible, so the best model is the one that is cheap to buy, easy for a first-time rider, and simple to keep running. For most shops that means a 110cc gearless scooty. A 350cc bike earns about two to three times the daily rent, but costs about twice as much to buy and attracts a different customer.

The numbers below cover petrol vehicles only. Before you buy anything for hire, also read how to get a bike rental licence, because renting out self-drive vehicles has its own rules.

What the table means for a rental shop

  • TVS Jupiter: lowest price and biggest storage. A 33-litre box holds a helmet and a small bag, which tourists ask about. Good first buy.
  • Honda Activa: the lowest seat (764 mm), so easiest for shorter riders and women to handle. Smallest storage of the three.
  • Suzuki Access 125: more engine for two people and for slopes, but the lowest claimed mileage and the tallest seat (856 mm), which shorter riders find hard at traffic lights. It also costs the most of the three.

The commuter bikes are cheap to run and to repair. The 350cc bikes bring in more per day but need more care: they are heavier, they are costlier to repair after a fall, and they are the first thing a first-time rider damages. If you add them, keep a clear security deposit policy and a written rental agreement.

How to choose: five checks

  1. Price against rent: divide the ex-showroom price by the daily rent. Jupiter at ₹74,400 and ₹499 a day is about 149 rental days to cover the price. Hunter at ₹1,37,640 and ₹1,000 a day (a cautious rate) is about 138 days.
  2. Who rides it: tourists want storage and an easy seat; local workers want low rent and mileage. Match the model to the customer, not to what looks good in a showroom.
  3. Seat height and weight: a first-time rider who cannot put a foot down will drop the vehicle. The 764 mm Activa seat and 856 mm Access seat differ by nearly 10 cm.
  4. Servicing near you: a vehicle that waits two weeks for a part earns nothing. Ask your dealer how long common parts take and where the nearest service centre is.
  5. Resale and insurance: ask your insurer about commercial rental cover before you buy, and ask the dealer what a one-year-old rental vehicle fetches. We do not have a reliable public figure for either, so get a quote.

What a rental scooty or bike can earn

This is a worked example, not a forecast. It uses the Bangalore operator's rate for a TVS Jupiter (₹499 a day) and a cautious ₹1,000 a day for a Hunter 350. It assumes ₹1,000 a month for servicing, insurance and small repairs on the scooty and ₹1,500 on the bike. Those cost figures are our assumptions: a consumer cost guide (April 2025) put a private scooter at about ₹250 servicing and ₹100 insurance a month, and rental vehicles work harder.

Treat the months as the best case.

The lesson is that days rented matters more than which model you pick: the same scooty takes 21 months at 9 days a month and under 8 months at 21 days. To keep days rented high, price weekly and monthly rentals sensibly, answer enquiries quickly, and keep every vehicle ready to go out. A sensible first fleet

If you are buying five vehicles, this is a starting mix. It is our suggestion, not a rule, and you should change it to match who walks into your shop:

  • Two Jupiter or Activa scooties for everyday and budget customers.
  • One Access 125 for taller riders and two-person rides.
  • One commuter motorcycle (Shine or Glamour class) for customers who want a bike without the price of a 350.
  • One Royal Enfield 350 only if you serve tourists or weekend riders. Wait to add a second until the first is booked most weekends.

Keep each vehicle's insurance, pollution certificate and permit dates in one place. RentalNow tracks insurance, PUC, permit and fitness expiry dates for every vehicle in your fleet, so a lapsed paper does not stop a booking. See bike and scooty rental software.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying the cheapest vehicle only. A model with poor spare availability costs you rental days.
  • Buying all one model. If a part or a recall affects it, your whole fleet is off the road together.
  • Ignoring who rides. A tall seat or a heavy bike in the hands of a first-time rider is a damage claim waiting to happen. Check the licence and ID before you hand over keys: how to verify customers.
  • Trusting claimed mileage. Claimed figures are test results. Your customers ride in traffic, with a pillion, and often with the throttle wide open.
  • Skipping the legal basics. Renting out vehicles for self-drive needs the right licence and permits; read how to obtain a bike rental licence first, and also see running bikes, scooties and cars together.

Questions owners ask

Which scooty is best for a rental business in India?

For most shops the TVS Jupiter or Honda Activa is the safest first buy: both are 110cc, claim about 59 to 61 km per litre, and cost about ₹74,000 to ₹76,000 ex-showroom. Jupiter has the larger under-seat box (33 litres against 18). Add a Suzuki Access 125 if you serve taller riders or pillion riders.

Is a scooty or a bike more profitable to rent out?

A 350cc bike earns more per day (₹1,200 to ₹1,400 in the listings we checked, against ₹499 to ₹599 for a scooty), but it costs ₹1.38 lakh to ₹2.16 lakh ex-showroom against about ₹75,000 for a scooty. In the worked example below both pay back in about a year at 50% utilisation, so the safer choice is the one your customers actually ask for.

How many days a month must a rental scooty be rented to pay for itself?

In the example below, a ₹74,400 scooty rented for 15 days a month at ₹499 pays back its price in about 11.5 months, and at 9 days a month in about 21 months. Utilisation matters more than the model.
