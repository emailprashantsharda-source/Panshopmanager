Best Car for a Rental Business in India

Which car should a self-drive rental business buy first? Compare the most rented models on price, mileage, space and daily rent, and see how long each takes to recover its cost.

The short answer

The best car for a rental business is the one that is rented on the most days, costs least to keep on the road, and holds its value when you sell it. For self-drive rental in India that usually means a small Maruti hatchback or compact sedan: cheap to buy, cheap to service, and easy to repair almost anywhere. Bigger cars earn more per day but cost more to buy and to fix, and they only pay when there is steady demand for them.

This guide covers the choice of model. For the licence, paperwork and the full cost sheet of a car rental business, read how to start a car rental business in India first. This guide covers petrol cars only.

What this means for a rental fleet

  • WagonR: the lowest price and a roomy boot for its size. Good for the budget customer and for city use.
  • Swift: the best claimed mileage and a popular name with customers, but the smallest boot of the three. A family with two large bags will notice.
  • Dzire: the biggest boot (382 L) and a sedan look at a small-car price. It is the easiest car to rent to families and to business travellers, and it rents for more than a Swift in the listing we read.

Other hatchbacks such as the Hyundai Grand i10 and i20 and the Tata Tiago also appear in rental listings. We use the Maruti models for the numbers because their prices and specifications are the easiest to check in one place.

Family cars and SUVs

Larger cars bring in more per day, and some customers will ask for nothing else. They are not the safest first purchase.

  • Maruti Ertiga: seven seats, ₹8.80 to ₹12.94 lakh ex-showroom (Delhi, after the GST change), and a claimed 20.3 km/l. It is the natural choice for families and groups, and costs far less than an Innova. Boot space is not listed on the comparison page we read.
  • Hyundai Creta and Kia Seltos: mid-size SUVs that renters book for road trips. They appear at the top of the rate ranges below. Ask local dealers for the current price before you plan around them.
  • Mahindra Thar: a lifestyle vehicle that tourists book for hill and beach trips. In the Goa listing a Thar rents for ₹2,353 a day and the automatic convertible for ₹3,448.
  • Toyota Innova Crysta and Hycross: seven-seat people movers rented for family and group travel, at about ₹3,500 a day in the same listing.

The 40% GST rate that now applies to larger cars (per CarOnPhone) can make an SUV or an Innova cost far more than a small car, so check the on-road price with a dealer before you work out your numbers.

Three things stand out. First, the three cars pay back at almost the same speed, because a dearer car also rents for more. Second, days rented matters far more than the model: a Dzire rented 21 days a month recovers its price in about two years, but at 9 days it takes over six. Third, a car takes about three years to repay itself at half-time use, against roughly one year for a scooty (see the best scooty and bike for rental guide). Buy a car only if you can keep it for years and keep it busy.

The real figure is worse, because registration, permit fees and accessories add to the price, and the table leaves out theft, accident damage, the days a car is in the workshop, staff and loan interest. Use the table to compare models, and use your own cost sheet to decide whether to buy at all.

How to choose: five checks

  1. Demand in your town: ask hotels, travel desks and your own enquiries what people ask for. A Thar suits a beach or hill town; a Dzire suits an airport or business city.
  2. Price against rent: divide the price by the daily rent. A Dzire at ₹6.24 lakh and ₹1,529 a day needs about 408 rental days just to cover the price; a WagonR needs about 416 and an Ertiga about 419.
  3. Servicing near you: a car waiting for a part earns nothing. Ask a dealer how fast common parts arrive and where the nearest service centre is. One operator guide describes the Dzire as having one of the largest service networks in India, but check your own town.
  4. Boot and seats: families and tourists carry luggage. The Dzire boot is 382 L, the Swift 265 L: the difference decides who rents which car.
  5. Resale and insurance: get a quote for commercial rental insurance and ask a dealer what a two-year-old rental car fetches. We found no reliable public figure for either.

A sensible first fleet

If you are buying three to five cars, this mix is our suggestion, not a rule:

  • Two Dzire or Swift cars, one manual and one automatic, for everyday customers.
  • One WagonR or other budget hatchback for price-sensitive customers.
  • One Ertiga for families, added once your first cars are booked most weekends.
  • An SUV or Innova last, and only when customers keep asking for it.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Buying an SUV first. It earns more per day but sits idle longer, and costs more to repair after an accident.
  • Buying one model only. If it needs a part or has a recall, your whole fleet stops together.
  • Ignoring the licence rules. Self-drive rental needs the right permit in your state. The 1989 scheme text asks for 50 cabs, and states differ, so confirm with your RTO. See our licence checklist.
  • Handing over keys without checks. Check the licence and ID, and take a deposit and a signed agreement: how to verify customers and the rental agreement generator.
  • Trusting claimed mileage and rents. Test figures and one operator's rates are a starting point, not a promise.

Questions owners ask

Which car is best for a self-drive rental business in India?

For most new operators the Maruti Dzire or Swift is the safest first car: they cost about ₹5.8 to ₹9.3 lakh ex-showroom, claim about 25 km per litre, and rent for about ₹1,300 to ₹1,500 a day in the Goa listing we read. Add an Ertiga for families and an SUV such as a Creta or Thar only where tourists ask for one.

Is a hatchback, sedan or SUV more profitable to rent out?

SUVs earn the most per day (₹2,500 to ₹4,000 in the rate ranges we read, against ₹1,000 to ₹1,800 for a hatchback), but they also cost the most to buy and to repair. In the worked example, hatchbacks, sedans and the Ertiga all take about three years to recover their price at 15 rental days a month, so days rented matters more than the model.

How many days a month must a rental car be rented to pay for itself?

In the example below, a Dzire at ₹1,529 a day takes about 37 months to recover its ₹6.24 lakh price at 15 rental days a month, and about 24 months at 21 days. At 9 days a month it takes more than six years. The figures leave out loan interest, theft, accidents and downtime.

Do I need a licence to rent out cars for self-drive?

Yes. Self-drive rental is regulated under the Motor Vehicles Act through the Rent a Cab Scheme, 1989, and each state runs its own rules. The central scheme text asks for a fleet of at least 50 cabs, and states have changed that over time, so ask your RTO for the current rule before you buy a car.

Are petrol, CNG or electric cars better for rental?

Customers usually pay for fuel, so a rental operator cares more about price, servicing and resale than mileage. Maruti lists CNG variants of the WagonR, Swift and Dzire. We did not find reliable public cost data for electric cars in rental use, so this guide compares petrol models.
